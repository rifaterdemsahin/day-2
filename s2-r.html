🔬 S2-R Research

Start here. The script explained beat by beat with diagrams, what is true and what is simplified, and why this topic matters to anyone who uses AI.

🧭 The idea in one paragraph

A language model does arithmetic, not reading. Before it can do anything with the word “king”, the word has to become numbers. An embedding is the rule for that: every token gets a long list of numbers (a point in space) arranged so that similar meanings sit close together. This one idea sits under search, chatbots, recommendations and RAG.

🎬 The script, beat by beat

🎬 Clip c1 · 0–8.8 s · 🗣️ “Tokens are just numbers. This one, nine-two-four-six, is a word. But to the AI, it means nothing yet.”

🔢 Tokens are just numbers

Text is first chopped into tokens (whole words or pieces of words) and each token gets an integer ID from a fixed vocabulary. ID 9246 is only a row number in a dictionary, like a page number: it says which word it is and nothing about what the word means. 9246 is not special; it is the hidden detail the loop returns to.

⚖️ Honest note: Real token IDs depend on the tokenizer. Our 9246 is an illustrative ID, not a claim about a specific model.

🎬 Clip c2 · 8.8–14.6 s · 🗣️ “So it gets unfolded. One number becomes fifteen hundred and thirty-six coordinates.”

🌌 One number becomes 1,536 coordinates

The model keeps a big table, the embedding matrix, with one row per token. Looking up ID 9246 returns that row: a vector of many decimal numbers. Each number is one coordinate, so the row is a point in a space with that many dimensions. 1,536 is a real size: OpenAI's text-embedding-3-small and ada-002 produce 1,536 numbers per text.

⚖️ Honest note: Different models use different sizes (hundreds to thousands). The individual coordinates have no human label such as “royalty”; the meaning is in the pattern across all of them.

🎬 Clip c3 · 14.6–23.9 s · 🗣️ “And meaning becomes distance. Apples, pears, grapes gather here. Kings, queens, crowns, over there.”

🧲 Meaning becomes distance

The numbers are not random. They are learned during training so that words used in similar contexts end up close together. Fruit words appear around “eat”, “ripe”, “juice”; royal words appear around “throne”, “crown”, “rule”. Closeness is measured with simple geometry (distance, or the angle between two vectors, called cosine similarity). That is why a search for “cheap flights” can find “budget airfare”.

⚖️ Honest note: We draw 2 dimensions because screens are flat. The real space has 1,536, and the picture is a squashed shadow of it.

🎬 Clip c4 · 23.9–29.1 s · 🗣️ “Now watch. Take king. Subtract man. Add woman.”

➕ King − man + woman

Because directions in the space carry meaning, you can do arithmetic on meaning. “King − man” removes the male part and leaves roughly “royalty”. Adding “woman” puts a female part back. The step from man to woman is a direction, and the same step moves king to queen.

⚖️ Honest note: This is the famous result from word2vec (Mikolov et al., 2013). It works best on classic word embeddings, and the check usually skips the input words themselves when it looks for the nearest word.

🎬 Clip c5 · 29.1–35 s · 🗣️ “And you land exactly on queen. No one told it. It's just geometry.”

👑 And you land on queen

Nobody wrote a rule “queen is the female king”. The model only practised predicting words from their neighbours across huge amounts of text. “The king wore a crown” and “the queen wore a crown” share contexts, so king and queen end up near each other, and the male-to-female step turns out to be the same step everywhere. Structure appears as a side effect of prediction.

⚖️ Honest note: “Exactly” is dramatic license: you land very close to queen, and queen is usually the nearest word, not a perfect match. For the short we say it plainly; this page keeps the honest version.

🎬 Clip c6 · 35–37.7 s · 🗣️ “But how do these vectors talk to each other?”

🕸️ How do vectors talk to each other?

An embedding gives each word a fixed starting point, but “bank” in “river bank” and “bank account” needs different meanings. The next layer, attention, lets every word's vector look at the others and pull in the context that matters. That is the Day 3 teaser, and the question loops back to the first line.

⚖️ Honest note: Attention was introduced in 2017 (“Attention Is All You Need”). Day 3 can show it as vectors pulling on each other.

🌍 Why this topic matters

  1. 🔎 Search that understands meaning
    Semantic search compares embeddings, so “car won't start” finds “engine trouble” even with no shared word.
  2. 📚 RAG and chat with your documents
    Documents are embedded and stored; the question is embedded; the closest chunks are handed to the model. No embeddings, no RAG.
  3. 🎯 Recommendations and matching
    Songs, products and people can be embedded too. Nearby points are things you will likely like.
  4. 🧱 The first layer of every LLM
    Every language model starts by turning tokens into vectors. Understanding this is the entry ticket to understanding attention, transformers and everything after.
  5. 🛡️ Why models are biased and how to fix it
    Directions in the space can encode stereotypes learned from text. You can only audit that if you know the space exists.
  6. 💸 It is cheap and practical
    Embedding a page costs a fraction of a cent, which is why this is the most used AI building block in production.
🎯 The takeaway for the viewer

Meaning in AI is geometry: close points mean similar things, and directions mean relationships. Once you see that, search, chatbots and recommendations stop being magic.
